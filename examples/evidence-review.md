# Research-step review fixtures

Timeboxed primary-source search, 6 October 2026. These are reviewer examples, not claims to bake into the product or a systematic/latest-literature review. Keep the speaker's personal experience separate from external population claims. A related study is not necessarily evidence for the exact statement.

## Five source records

1. **Software engineers: limited population match.** [Verma, Cruz and Liebel, 2025, Differences between Neurodivergent and Neurotypical Software Engineers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03840). This research preprint analyses 2022 Stack Overflow survey responses from professional engineers, including ADHD (n=1,305), autism (n=374) and dyslexia (n=363). ADHD respondents reported more interruptions while awaiting answers and less frequent interaction outside their team. This is self-reported, selected developer-survey data, not a representative estimate of neurodivergence across technology workers or startup founders. Publication year is not data-collection year. It does not measure public thought leadership, special interests, or short-post composition.

2. **Entrepreneur percentage: support only with strong qualification.** [Sônego et al., online 2020 / journal issue 2021, Exploring the association between ADHD and entrepreneurship](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8023171/). Of 259 Brazilian entrepreneur-course applicants with complete questionnaires, 71 (27.4%) met the study's self-report symptom-and-impairment criteria for ADHD-positive status. The initial invitation response rate was 10.8%; complete analyses covered approximately 6% of invited people. This is a selected sample and screening classification, not a clinical diagnostic survey or a population prevalence estimate. It does not establish that 27.4% of startup founders, technology employees, or all entrepreneurs have ADHD; it does not cover neurodivergence as a whole.

3. **Writing fluency: related evidence, not a social-post timer.** [Reactivity of the triple task on writing processes and product in adults with dyslexia, 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1112274/full). Forty Croatian-speaking adults aged 18–38 (20 with developmental dyslexia and 20 typical readers) wrote narrative texts in two conditions, with a 25-minute limit per text. The dyslexia group produced fewer characters per minute and took more breaks relative to active writing. Small sample, one language, narrative tasks, and a study designed primarily to examine measurement reactivity. Do not transform this into a percentage increase in time to write a tweet, email, or professional post. It neither measures ADHD nor establishes that non-linear thinking causes these differences.

4. **AI writing support: relevant design evidence.** [Goodman et al., ASSETS 2022, LaMPost](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02308), [conference DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3517428.3544819). Nineteen adults with dyslexia evaluated a prototype with outlining, subject-line generation, suggested changes, and selection rewriting. Rewriting and subject-line features were promising, but generated text did not consistently meet the accuracy and quality needed by participants. This is a small prototype evaluation, not proof that this app saves time or that every person with dyslexia benefits from AI. It supports retaining user review and editable suggestions.

5. **ADHD knowledge work: reported needs, not measured writing-time difference.** [Campbell, Çerçi and Cecchinato, 2024, ADHD and Knowledge Work](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/adhd-and-knowledge-work-exploring-strategies-challenges-and-oppor/), DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-61688-4_10. Online qualitative survey of 49 UK knowledge workers self-identifying with ADHD: 20 reported a diagnosis, 12 were waiting, and 17 self-assessed. Participants described prioritisation, time-estimation and task-switching challenges; AI users reported help progressing tasks. Small self-selected survey with mixed diagnostic status, no timed comparison, and no causal efficacy claim. Publication was July 2024; the conference was in 2023.

## Expected review behaviour

- “27.4% of tech founders are neurodivergent.” → **Needs qualification / population mismatch.** The entrepreneur paper cannot establish this claim. Offer its actual sample-specific result separately; do not silently replace “tech founders” with “entrepreneurs.”
- “People with ADHD take twice as long to write a 250-character post.” → **No direct evidence found in this search.** Do not invent a ratio or convert narrative typing measures into post-writing time. Lack of evidence found is not evidence that the claim is false.
- “I find it harder to get my thoughts into a short post.” → **Personal experience.** Preserve it without demanding a clinical citation. Do not generalise to everyone with the same diagnosis.
- “Research suggests some adults with dyslexia face additional demands in written composition.” → **Related evidence, scope matters.** Show the exact adult narrative-task study and its small sample; let the user accept a qualified phrasing.
- “Neurodivergent people have deeper interests but struggle to become thought leaders.” → **Split the claim.** Interest intensity, writing, public communication, and professional opportunity are separate outcomes. These sources do not establish the combined statement.

Each evidence card should retain claim text, source URL/title/date, study population, measured outcome, relevance, limitations, and proposed wording. Keep external evidence visibly separate from transcript excerpts. Only user-accepted evidence may be added to drafts, with inline source links or a compact source list. If evidence is missing, preserve uncertainty or omit the generalisation; never manufacture a citation.
